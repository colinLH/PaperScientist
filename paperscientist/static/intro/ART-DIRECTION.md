# Cosmic opening

The user's local `web-designs` collection is the visual reference, viewed at port 8930 and read from its source files:

- `099-aurora-canvas-scene`: layered light curtains for the first chapter and the WORK environment.
- `138-dot-globe-network`: a rotating point globe with travelling connection arcs for the second chapter.
- `195-solar-system-scene`: orbiting bodies and a warm central star for the third chapter. The user's supplied gravity-well image informs the tilted fabric underneath.
- `107-gradient-orb-hero`: a soft spherical light field behind WORK, recoloured in cool blue and silver.

These are independent procedural illustrations, without their marketing copy, invented live metrics, external scripts or permanent animation loops. The globe has decorative approximate land silhouettes; the orbital scene compresses geometry and invents periods. Neither is a geographic or astronomical data product.

The design-taste direction draw selected **One motif**, seed `756135611`: follow a research question from a wave, through connected knowledge, into an experiment, and finally a paper project. Its drawn burgundy/pink palette conflicts with the user's explicit no-red-background request and is replaced by navy, teal, silver and a warm star. Its stock sections are superseded by the requested existing seven-chapter journey. Luminous graphic lines, gradients and particles are intentional user requirements. The shared sans-serif opening copy occupies two lines, with one large subject per frame. Existing navigation and the WORK silhouette, lettering and focus/reverse camera remain intact.

The opening uses one bounded 2D canvas and the existing journey clock. Fixed samples: 240 stars and 2,400 globe points. Hidden opening chapters skip all drawing; the underlying landscape WebGL renderer is also skipped while the opening or another opaque scene covers it. Reduced motion fixes time; resize preserves samples. No image assets or packages are added. Motion is for this occasional introductory experience, not routine agent interaction.

The final review uses the project's real browser through CUA at desktop, tablet and narrow widths. The skill's CDP-driven inspector is not used because browser control is restricted to CUA. A visual pass checks reading order, contrast, composition, continuity, clipping, reverse travel and a quiet static mode; unit tests cover resource budgets and deterministic geometry. Browser-only timings must not be described as universal GPU performance guarantees.
