# Welcome Work gallery

Motion reference: the Work chapter of [Wodniack](https://wodniack.dev/), checked against the user's recording: vertical WORK capsule, concave repeated letter wall, then the reverse capsule exit. This is an independent implementation. No website source, proprietary fonts or recordings are bundled.

The new user recording dated 2026-10-26 is the motion reference for the entrance and exit. A shared, reversible camera path first pushes the capsule closer, then unfolds the compressed WORK columns horizontally. On exit the columns fold back before the camera pulls away; the capsule stays centered instead of sliding upward. Existing easing and spring mechanics are reused, with a slower response for the portal and the faster response retained for gallery scrolling.

The gallery opens on an empty letter wall. Its first 4% of scroll progress is a lead-in (roughly two to three third-viewport gestures, depending on viewport), then the first curtain enters from the right on the lower rail. The next curtain enters on the opposite rail when the outgoing curtain’s right edge is one third of a viewport from the left edge. Twelve official paper previews alternate lower/upper rails, with at most two visible across the screen including partial edges. They finish exiting by 96% of gallery progress, leaving a clear wall before closure. No timer drives entry, exit, or replacement: stopping preserves positions, and reversing retraces the same sequence. All twelve previews remain accessible through the numbered navigation.

Cross-page wheel input cannot accumulate destinations while the current page is approaching. After arriving near its destination, the camera has a 180 ms breathing space, and a fresh wheel gesture is required (320 ms quiet gap outside Work). Ignored input is consumed rather than queued, while an intentional mid-flight reversal remains possible. Inside Work, each wheel/trackpad gesture advances one third of the viewport width. Large deltas and trailing inertia are consumed without queuing extra steps (180 ms quiet gap distinguishes gestures). Small deltas accumulate to an 18 px threshold. A fresh opposite-direction gesture steps back by the same distance immediately; the spring retains velocity. The curtain distance and repeated WORK wall phase share the same travel distance; the letter wall converts it into its existing perspective column pitch. Entrance and exit retain boundary protection. Reduced-motion snapping preserves the gesture lock without depending on animation frames. The critically damped spring preserves velocity when reversed.

These are official project media previews, not live embedded websites. Third-party websites, scripts and tracking code are not executed inside the gallery. Only the central preview in each row plays. Other cards show covers. Leaving the scene, opening the credits dialog, hiding the tab, or pausing previews unloads video sources. Late playback promises cannot restart an inactive card or pause a newly reactivated card. Network failures and autoplay denial fall back to the cover/source link; the pause/play control allows an explicit retry. Reduced motion leaves previews static unless the user enables playback. Public media URLs require network access and may change independently of this repository.

The letter wall is one 2D canvas using a cached glyph sprite instead of many transformed SVG nodes. Its drawing buffer is capped near 2.2 million pixels. Work targets 60 scene updates per second during movement. The closed portal shows an independent orbital gateway: fourteen inclined bands, travelling highlights, perspective meridians and 420 fixed stars. It shares the atlas’s cool night palette on entry and warms on exit toward the final research harbor. Its bands contract with the same focus camera; it no longer repeats the opening’s light ribbons. WORK glyphs, wall geometry and previews retain their existing design. The field fades away during unfolding. The open gallery stops repainting when settled; videos use their own browser decoder clocks. No extra WebGL context or postprocessing framebuffer is created. The original six SVG illustrations remain as bundled artwork, but are not used by the new paper previews.

The seven chapters keep their eleven navigation stops: question stream → knowledge sphere → experiment orbits → warp → nebula → Work capsule → gallery (third-viewport steps between stops 6 and 8) → capsule exit → research harbor. The original nebula, meteors, camera, hands and music remain in their established renderer. The warp retains 1200 projected stars, five speed levels and its 2.6-million-pixel cap.

The user-provided local `142-gravity-particles` demo informed the attraction/orbit motif. This implementation has no independent physics loop, no accumulating trail framebuffer and no third-party scripts. Reduced motion freezes the field, and hidden scenes do not draw. The warp now uses straight projected light trails again, retaining the dark central well and elliptical backdrop. This is visual design, not a general-relativity simulation. The nebula texture and meteor shader are unchanged. The star atlas now has 18 nodes and 11 short links in disconnected groups across all four screen quadrants, attached to the camera plane so wide and narrow viewports preserve their distribution. It retains the native-up-to-3840×2160 settled rendering policy. Eleven travelling signals reuse a fixed buffer; resize and disposal cover every path and point resource.

The first three chapters morph the same 2400 samples and six light filaments from a question stream into a globe and then laboratory orbits. There is no scene respawn or crossfade between separate artworks; both directions evaluate the same progress function. Their narrative copy follows question → clues → direction → evidence. The same capped canvas is reused for the final harbor: orbital instruments, satellites, terraced observatories, dishes, lit windows, a bridge and motes over the existing dawn landscape. It is hidden throughout the middle chapters. No independent timer, framebuffer, image bundle or dependency is added. See `../ART-DIRECTION.md`.
