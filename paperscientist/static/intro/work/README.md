# Welcome Work gallery

Motion reference: the Work chapter of [Wodniack](https://wodniack.dev/), checked against the user's recording: vertical WORK capsule, concave repeated letter wall, then the reverse capsule exit. This is an independent implementation. No website source, proprietary fonts or recordings are bundled.

The new user recording dated 2026-10-26 is the motion reference for the entrance and exit. A shared, reversible camera path first pushes the capsule closer, then unfolds the compressed WORK columns horizontally. On exit the columns fold back before the camera pulls away; the capsule stays centered instead of sliding upward. Existing easing and spring mechanics are reused, with a slower response for the portal and the faster response retained for gallery scrolling.

The gallery opens on an empty letter wall. Its first 9% of scroll progress is a lead-in (roughly two to three third-viewport gestures, depending on viewport), then the first curtain enters from the right on the lower rail. The next curtain enters on the opposite rail only once 70% of the outgoing curtain is past the left edge. Six official paper previews alternate lower/upper rails, with at most two visible across the screen including partial edges. They finish exiting by 91% of gallery progress, leaving a clear wall before closure. No timer drives entry, exit, or replacement: stopping preserves positions, and reversing retraces the same sequence. All six previews remain accessible through the numbered navigation.

Cross-page wheel input cannot accumulate destinations while the current page is approaching. After arriving near its destination, the camera has a 180 ms breathing space, and a fresh wheel gesture is required (320 ms quiet gap outside Work). Ignored input is consumed rather than queued, while an intentional mid-flight reversal remains possible. Inside Work, each wheel/trackpad gesture advances one third of the viewport width. Large deltas and trailing inertia are consumed without queuing extra steps (180 ms quiet gap distinguishes gestures). Small deltas accumulate to an 18 px threshold. A fresh opposite-direction gesture steps back by the same distance immediately; the spring retains velocity. The curtain distance and repeated WORK wall phase share the same travel distance; the letter wall converts it into its existing perspective column pitch. Entrance and exit retain boundary protection. Reduced-motion snapping preserves the gesture lock without depending on animation frames. The critically damped spring preserves velocity when reversed.

The six previews link to official research project sites. Each mini page uses the project's title, venue, cover and a muted, looping demonstration video. These are official project media previews, not live embedded websites. Third-party websites, scripts and tracking code are not executed inside the gallery. Media stay on their authors' servers and retain their original ownership:

Only the central preview in each row plays. Other cards show covers. Leaving the scene, opening the credits dialog, hiding the tab, or pausing previews unloads video sources. Late playback promises cannot restart an inactive card or pause a newly reactivated card. Network failures and autoplay denial fall back to the cover/source link; the pause/play control allows an explicit retry. Reduced motion leaves previews static unless the user enables playback. Public media URLs require network access and may change independently of this repository.

The letter wall is one 2D canvas using a cached glyph sprite instead of many transformed SVG nodes. Its drawing buffer is capped near 2.2 million pixels. Work targets 60 scene updates per second during movement. The closed portal shows a deep-blue light field combining the local 099 aurora and 107 light-orb references, with 420 fixed star particles at at most 30 updates/second; light curtains and a cool spherical halo contract with the shared camera. The previous red perspective grid is removed. WORK glyphs, wall geometry and previews retain their existing design. The field fades away during unfolding. The open gallery stops repainting when settled; videos use their own browser decoder clocks. No extra WebGL context or postprocessing framebuffer is created. The original six SVG illustrations remain as bundled artwork, but are not used by the new paper previews.

The seven chapters keep their eleven navigation stops: aurora → knowledge globe → orbital laboratory → warp → nebula → Work capsule → gallery (third-viewport steps between stops 6 and 8) → capsule exit → dawn. The original nebula, meteors, camera, hands and music remain in their established renderer. The warp retains 400 projected stars, five speed levels and its 2.6-million-pixel cap.

The user-provided local `142-gravity-particles` demo informed the attraction/orbit motif. This implementation has no independent physics loop, no accumulating trail framebuffer and no third-party scripts. Reduced motion freezes the field, and hidden scenes do not draw. The warp now uses straight projected light trails again, retaining the dark central well and elliptical backdrop. This is visual design, not a general-relativity simulation. The nebula texture and meteor shader are unchanged. The star atlas is new vector geometry: 16 nodes, four highlighted hubs with orbital ticks, 21 primary routes and 10 secondary links. It uses the existing native-up-to-3840×2160 settled rendering policy and releases its geometry/materials with the scene.

The nebula atlas also drifts gently around its own centre, with 21 light signals travelling along the main routes and breathing navigation rings. Signals reuse one fixed GPU buffer. Reduced motion uses a fixed time for the full scene; the original nebula texture and meteor implementation remain unchanged. See `../ART-DIRECTION.md` for the new opening chapters.
